## When to use which scale?

- **HRC (Rockwell C)**: the most common scale on engineering drawings for hardened parts. Useful range: 20–68 HRC.
  Fast, non-destructive measurement — ideal for tool steels, case-hardened or nitrided parts.
- **HB (Brinell)**: recommended for softer or heterogeneous materials (cast irons, annealed steels,
  aluminium alloys). The large ball (Ø 10 mm) averages structural heterogeneities.
- **HV (Vickers)**: universal — valid from very low to very high hardness values. Tiny indentation:
  the only scale usable on thin surface layers (nitriding, case hardening, coatings).
  Reference scale for thermochemical treatment standards.
- **Rm (MPa)**: tensile strength is derived from hardness via empirical correlation (table A.1, ISO 18265).
  Indicative value only — it does not replace a tensile test.

## Conversion limitations

ISO 18265 states that these correspondences are **approximate** and valid only for unalloyed and
low-alloy steels in the quenched and tempered condition. They do not apply to:

- austenitic stainless steels,
- high-speed steels (HSS),
- cast irons,
- non-ferrous alloys (aluminium, copper, titanium),
- cemented carbides.

Specific tables exist for these materials (annexes B to E of the standard).

| Material / condition | Typical HRC | Typical HB | Typical HV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annealed steel (e.g. 42CrMo4 annealed) | — | 180–220 | 185–225 |
| Quenched & tempered steel (42CrMo4 QT) | 30–38 | 285–360 | 295–370 |
| Hardened tool steel (100Cr6) | 58–64 | — | 650–780 |
| Nitrided steel (compound layer) | — | — | 700–1000 |
| Case-hardened & quenched (16MnCr5) | 58–62 | — | 690–750 |
