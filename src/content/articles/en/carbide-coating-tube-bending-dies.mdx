During tube bending, the workpiece is held by **clamp dies** that must prevent it from slipping
throughout the entire cycle. On a high-speed CNC bender, the forces involved are significant —
and a smooth metal-on-metal contact surface simply is not enough. The tube slips, the bend
shifts, the part is rejected.

The solution we use at LIP Industrie Précision for tooling supplied to **Alfa Tube** machines:
depositing a layer of **tungsten carbide** on the contact area of the die. Not to harden the
part — but to create **grip**.

## The problem: a polished die slips

A die machined from heat-treated steel has a relatively smooth surface. That is a quality for
geometric precision — but a liability for clamping. Under the tension and push forces of the
bending cycle, the tube tries to escape. On thin-wall tubes or slippery materials (stainless
steel, aluminium), the effect is even more pronounced.

Classical solutions — grooves, milled teeth — risk marking the tube and creating stress
concentrations. The carbide deposit provides grip **without damaging** the tube surface.

## The process: electro spark deposition (ESD)

<Image src={imgRocklinizer} alt="Rocklinizer electro spark deposition machine for tungsten carbide coating — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" title="Rocklinizer — ESD carbide deposition unit, LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" />

The process is called **ESD** — *Electro Spark Deposition*. The machine generates controlled
electrical pulses between a tungsten carbide electrode and the steel surface. With each spark,
a tiny amount of carbide is **transferred and metallurgically bonded** to the workpiece surface.

The result is a granular, dense layer that does not peel off — unlike thermal spray or chemical
coatings. The deposited thickness is minimal (a few microns to a few hundredths of a millimetre),
so the functional dimensions of the part are not affected.

<Image src={imgApplication} alt="Applying carbide deposit on a tube bending die with the Rocklinizer applicator — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" title="Carbide deposition in progress on bending die — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" />

The process is carried out **cold**: no significant temperature rise, no risk of distortion or
annealing for hardened parts. The die can be treated **after** its final heat treatment and
machining.

## What it looks like on the part

<Image src={imgMorsGalet} alt="Circular tube bending die showing granular carbide deposit on the contact groove — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" title="Tube bending clamp die with carbide deposit — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" />

The deposit is visible to the naked eye: the treated area shows a **granular grey texture**,
clearly different from the surrounding polished metal. To the touch, it is rough — intentionally.
This microtopography creates mechanical grip points against the tube surface without deforming
or marking it.

On bending dies, the deposit is applied **only to the functional contact area** — the groove or
bearing surface that grips the tube. Mounting faces and seating surfaces are left untouched.

## Carbide vs nitriding: two distinct functions

<Image src={imgMorsNoirs} alt="Series of nitrided tube bending dies (black surface) without carbide deposit — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" title="Nitrided bending dies — LIP Industrie Précision, Besançon" />

The dies we manufacture for Alfa Tube sometimes receive **two different treatments depending
on their function**:

- **Nitriding** (black surface): a thermochemical treatment sub-contracted locally, which
  diffuses nitrogen into the surface to increase surface hardness and wear resistance.
  This is a **depth treatment** — a few tenths of a millimetre.
- **ESD carbide deposit**: applied in-house at LIP, targeted at the gripping area.
  Its function is **grip**, not hardness. The layer thickness is minimal.

Both treatments can coexist on the same part: nitride first for long-term wear resistance,
then apply carbide on the grip zone.

## Machining and surface treatment under one roof

At LIP Industrie Précision, dies are **designed, machined, and treated** within the same
project — in direct coordination with Alfa Tube, which defines the functional parameters of
the tooling (tube material, bending force, cycle rate). This eliminates back-and-forth between
subcontractors and ensures consistency between part geometry and surface treatment.

Nitriding is handled by local partners in Franche-Comté. The carbide deposit is done
**in-house** with our Rocklinizer unit.
